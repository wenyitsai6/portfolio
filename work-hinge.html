Usability study · HCDE 417 · Team of 5

Ten people. One missing button.

We ran think-aloud usability sessions on Hinge's new-user experience with ten participants. Every single one of them hit the same wall — you cannot go back during onboarding. It became the highest-priority fix in our report.

What we were asking

Hinge markets itself as "designed to be deleted" — a dating app built for intentional relationships. But getting to the app itself means answering 20+ demographic questions first, covering everything from age to political and religious belief.

We evaluated two things: the new-user experience through onboarding, and whether people could find and understand Dating Preferences and Dealbreakers — the controls that shape who you see and who sees you.

Who we tested with

Ten participants aged 18–29, split into two groups: eight who had never used Hinge, and two lapsed users inactive for six months or more. That split let us separate genuine first-run confusion from habits formed by prior use.

How we ran it

Each session was moderated 1:1 over Zoom with phone screen-sharing, one moderator and at least one notetaker. Participants thought aloud while completing tasks, then answered follow-up interview questions. We scored each task against a shared success-criteria table — from Success through Delayed Success to Failed — so results stayed comparable across different moderators.

Afterwards we open-coded the recordings and migrated everything to Miro for affinity diagramming: one sticky color per participant, ten categories, then grouped into themes.

Finding 01 — Onboarding is long, rigid, and overwhelming

This was the biggest section of the study and produced the most severe issues. Six separate problems compound into one bad first impression.

There is no back button. At all.

10 out of 10 participants were frustrated by this — the single most universal finding in the study. Moving forward is effortless; going back is impossible. One participant said it would make them consider deleting the app.

You can't, like, go back and adjust your preferences as you're doing it initially. You have to go through the whole onboarding and then adjust it later, which I think is… a little goofy.

P3

There's no clear back path to adjust previous answers during onboarding. [This feels] kinda annoying. I'm thinking negatively about that.

P4

Terminology people can't parse

Five of nine participants hit words they didn't understand — and the app offered no explanation. Two didn't know what monogamy and non-monogamy meant. Two couldn't tell life partner from long-term. One skipped the gender identity question entirely rather than answer something they didn't understand.

I'm not entirely sure what these mean. I'm just gonna assume monogamy is, like, two people in it… so maybe they should have the definition somewhere.

P5

Pop-ups fragment the flow

Permissions, hints and Hinge+ upsells interrupt setup repeatedly. Participants understood why they existed and still found them intrusive — one noted premium prompts appeared before they'd had any chance to evaluate the free tier.

I keep getting these pop-ups every two seconds. I'm gonna blow this app up, bro… I get that they're necessary, but they break up the flow.

P3

Skipping is possible but invisible

Two participants wanted to skip questions; only one worked out how. The skip control is so low-contrast that P8 assumed it didn't exist and tapped at random until something worked.

Oh, I can skip some questions, but it's not obvious that I can.

P1

Finding 02 — Dealbreakers are hard to find and harder to understand

Dating Preferences and Dealbreakers are how you control your match pool. Both fail at the first step: discovery.

  • Every participant prompted to find Dating Preferences needed multiple attempts, wandering between pages until they stumbled on it. The icon is small and gives no clue what it does.
  • 5 of 10 could not easily locate Dealbreaker options — they're scattered across individual preference categories rather than centralized anywhere.
  • 4 of 10 didn't understand what a Dealbreaker is. One thought it was a tiebreaker for choosing between similar profiles, rather than a filter that excludes people.
  • Hinge does display "Dealbreakers will limit who you see and who sees you" — none of those four noticed it.

The dating preference… finding that… I could find everything else but that. It's kind of like a small icon on the top left. You would not think that's that.

P6

I don't know, that one says Dealbreaker, and nothing else says Dealbreaker, so…

P8

Finding 03 — People can't tell what's free

Participants weren't hostile to premium features. They were frustrated by not knowing where the line was until they hit it.

  • 7 of 10 didn't realize how many features were premium-locked until they ran into them mid-exploration.
  • Two discovered that preferences they'd set during free onboarding — like Dating Intentions — couldn't be changed later without upgrading. The choice was made before anyone said it was permanent.
  • One participant found the swipe limit only by exhausting it. No counter, no warning.

Nope, that takes Hinge+. Dating intentions, that takes Hinge+. Active today, that takes Hinge+. Let me guess.

P8

What we recommended

We ranked every fix by frequency and severity — how many participants hit it, and how badly it blocked them. Prioritising by user count rather than engineering effort kept the conversation on impact.

The back button tops the list because it is the rare case where severity and reach line up perfectly: it affected everyone, it blocks error recovery entirely, and it is a small, well-understood piece of interface to add.

What I'd do differently

  • We used convenience and snowball sampling — most participants had an existing connection to someone on the team. That likely made people more comfortable than a stranger would have been, and more generous in their feedback.
  • Eight new users to two lapsed users is lopsided. A more even split would have said more about what brings someone back after six months away.
  • Everyone was 18–29. That's Hinge's core demographic, but it means we can say nothing about how the same onboarding reads to someone older.
  • Two sessions were disrupted by genuine app bugs. Useful data about reliability, but it cost us task time we'd planned for the dealbreaker section.
